# Rev Matched — Tone & Voice Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the tone, voice, and communication style for the Rev Matched platform.

The goal is to ensure consistency across:

- UI copy
- onboarding
- notifications
- marketing
- seller experiences
- customer support
- future AI/recommendation systems

Rev Matched should feel emotionally intelligent, human, and locally relatable.

The platform should never sound:

- cold
- overly corporate
- aggressive
- overly technical

## Brand Voice Summary

Rev Matched should sound like:

> a trusted friend helping someone navigate an important life decision.

The tone should feel:

- supportive
- modern
- confident
- emotionally aware
- conversational

The platform is guiding users through:

- excitement
- uncertainty
- comparison
- ownership
- transition

The voice should adapt to those emotional states while remaining consistent.

## Core Voice Characteristics

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Human | Sounds natural and conversational |
| Supportive | Helps users feel guided and reassured |
| Clear | Simple language over technical jargon |
| Calm | Never overwhelming or pushy |
| Warm | Emotionally approachable |
| Confident | Helpful without sounding uncertain |
| Locally Relevant | Reflects Trinidadian culture naturally |
| Playful (Lightly) | Occasional warmth and charm without becoming gimmicky |

## The Emotional Goal

Rev Matched should help users feel:

- less overwhelmed
- more confident
- more emotionally connected to the process
- more in control of their decisions

The platform should reduce:

- stress
- confusion
- decision fatigue

The voice should consistently reinforce:

> “You’re making progress.”

## Tone by Platform Experience

## 1. Find Your Match

### Emotional Tone

- optimistic
- exploratory
- exciting
- encouraging

This area should feel like:

> discovering possibilities rather than shopping inventory.

### Tone Examples

#### Good

- “Let’s find something that fits your life.”
- “A few new matches just rolled in.”
- “This one might suit your routine.”
- “Still exploring? Here are a few more possibilities.”

#### Avoid

- “Results Found”
- “Inventory Updated”
- “Vehicle Search Completed”

These sound mechanical and transactional.

## 2. Life Together

### Emotional Tone

- supportive
- dependable
- organized
- reassuring

This section should feel like:

> a trusted ownership companion.

### Tone Examples

#### Good

- “Your inspection date is coming up.”
- “Time for a quick tyre check?”
- “Here are a few nearby service options.”
- “Keeping things running smoothly.”

#### Avoid

- “Maintenance Alert”
- “Service Requirement Detected”
- “Critical Ownership Reminder”

These create unnecessary stress.

## 3. Moving On

### Emotional Tone

- smooth
- practical
- encouraging
- low-pressure

Selling should feel:

> like a guided transition rather than a stressful process.

### Tone Examples

#### Good

- “Ready for the next chapter?”
- “Let’s get your listing looking its best.”
- “A few buyers are showing interest.”
- “Here’s how similar cars are being priced.”

#### Avoid

- “Maximize Lead Conversion”
- “Optimize Listing Performance”
- “Aggressively Price Your Vehicle”

These sound sales-heavy and impersonal.

## Copywriting Principles

## Principle 1 — Speak Like a Human

Write the way a thoughtful person would naturally speak.

Avoid:

- robotic phrasing
- overly formal wording
- corporate jargon

### Example

#### Avoid

“Your selected preferences have been successfully updated.”

#### Better

“Your preferences have been updated.”

#### Even Better

“Got it — we’ll use these preferences going forward.”

## Principle 2 — Reduce Stress

Buying and selling cars can feel overwhelming.

The platform should calm users rather than pressure them.

Avoid:

- fear-based language
- urgency manipulation
- aggressive prompts

### Avoid

- “Act Now”
- “Don’t Miss Out”
- “Last Chance”

### Prefer

- “Take another look”
- “Worth considering”
- “Here are a few options nearby”

## Principle 3 — Reinforce Progress

Users should consistently feel:

- movement
- momentum
- narrowing clarity

The voice should subtly reinforce:

> “You’re getting closer.”

### Examples

- “You’ve narrowed things down nicely.”
- “A few strong contenders emerging.”
- “Your Top Picks are shaping up.”

## Principle 4 — Keep Language Simple

Use:

- shorter sentences
- familiar words
- direct communication

Avoid:

- technical terminology
- automotive jargon overload
- unnecessarily complex explanations

## Principle 5 — Emotion Before Technicality

Lead with:

- relevance
- confidence
- clarity

Technical details should support the emotional experience rather than dominate it.

## Local Tone & Cultural Relevance

Rev Matched should feel Trinidadian without becoming caricatured.

The platform should:

- naturally reflect local realities
- understand local driving culture
- occasionally reference familiar experiences subtly

Examples:

- traffic
- beach runs
- daily commuting
- rainy season driving
- doubles runs
- island road conditions

### Important Rule

Do not force slang into the experience.

The tone should feel:

- authentic
- modern
- natural

Avoid exaggerated “local” language that feels performative.

## UX Writing Style

### Preferred Style

- short
- clear
- calm
- emotionally intelligent

### Good Examples

#### Buttons

- Find Matches
- Save Changes
- Explore More
- Add to Top Picks
- Reconsider
- Compare Picks

#### Status Messaging

- “3 new matches available.”
- “You already have 3 Top Picks.”
- “Want to replace one?”
- “This car is back in your recommendations.”

#### Empty States

- “Nothing here yet — let’s fix that.”
- “You haven’t added any Top Picks yet.”
- “Still exploring? We’ll keep looking.”

### Avoid Overexplaining

The platform should feel lightweight and intuitive.

Avoid:

- large instructional paragraphs
- excessive tooltip dependence
- over-engineered explanations

Users should feel guided naturally.

## Notification Tone

Notifications should feel:

- useful
- relevant
- low-pressure

### Good Examples

- “A new match just landed.”
- “One of your Top Picks dropped in price.”
- “A few similar vehicles just appeared.”
- “Your inspection date is coming up.”

### Avoid

- “URGENT”
- “Immediate Action Required”
- “Critical Alert”

unless truly necessary.

## Error Messaging Tone

Errors should feel:

- calm
- clear
- constructive

Avoid blaming the user.

### Good Examples

- “Something went wrong. Try again in a moment.”
- “We couldn’t save that just yet.”
- “Looks like that listing is no longer available.”

### Avoid

- “Invalid Input”
- “Operation Failed”
- “Fatal Error”

## Seller Tone

Seller experiences should feel:

- guided
- encouraging
- transparent

The platform should avoid sounding:

- manipulative
- overly sales-focused
- technical

### Good Examples

- “A few small improvements could help this listing stand out.”
- “Cars with brighter photos usually get more attention.”
- “Here’s how similar vehicles are being priced.”

## Brand Personality Summary

If Rev Matched were a person, it would be:

- emotionally intelligent
- visually aware
- organized
- calm under pressure
- supportive
- modern
- quietly confident

It would help people think clearly without making them feel stupid or overwhelmed.

## Closing Principle

Every piece of communication in Rev Matched should reinforce the feeling that:

- the platform understands the user
- the process is becoming clearer
- they are moving toward a decision with greater confidence

The experience should feel:

> warm, modern, emotionally intelligent, and reassuring — never cold or transactional.
