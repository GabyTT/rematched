# Rev Matched — Terminology & Product Language Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the core terminology used throughout the Rev Matched platform.

The goal is to:

- ensure consistency across product, design, development, and marketing
- maintain emotionally intelligent UX language
- reduce ambiguity during implementation
- preserve the relationship-driven tone of the platform

This document should be treated as the canonical language reference for the project.

## Core Platform Structure

Rev Matched is organized around three major platform experiences:

| Experience | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Find Your Match | Buyer discovery and decision journey |
| Life Together | Ownership and maintenance journey |
| Moving On | Seller and transition journey |

## Core User Journey Terminology

### Define

The stage where users define:

- budget
- preferred vehicle types
- favourite brands/models
- lifestyle needs

Purpose:
Establish compatibility criteria before discovery begins.

### Discover

The stage where users browse compatible and unexplored vehicles curated by the platform.

Discover focuses on:

- exploration
- compatibility
- reducing overwhelm

Discover should primarily show:

- matched vehicles
- unrated vehicles
- recommendation-driven content

### Match

A Match is a system-generated compatibility result.

Definition:

> A vehicle that aligns with the user’s defined preferences.

Important:

- Match is not a user action.
- Matches are generated algorithmically.
- Users can Like vehicles that are not Matches.

Purpose:
Provide algorithmic reassurance and reduce decision fatigue.

### Like

A user-generated expression of interest in a vehicle.

Definition:

> “I am emotionally interested in this vehicle.”

Important:

- Users can Like matched or non-matched vehicles.
- Likes represent curiosity and attraction.
- Liked vehicles become comparison candidates.

### Pass

A user-generated rejection or dismissal of a vehicle.

Definition:

> “I am not interested in this vehicle right now.”

Important:

- Passed vehicles are excluded from active discovery.
- Passed vehicles may reappear in `Second Chances`.

Purpose:
Reduce repetitive discovery fatigue.

### Top Pick

A serious finalist selected by the user.

Definition:

> “This is one of my strongest contenders.”

Business Rules:

- Top Picks must first be Liked.
- Maximum of 3 active Top Picks.
- Selecting a 4th Top Pick prompts replacement of the oldest one.

Purpose:
Encourage intentional narrowing and reduce analysis paralysis.

## Discovery & Recommendation Terminology

### Match Score

A calculated compatibility score generated by the recommendation system.

Purpose:
Indicates how closely a vehicle aligns with user preferences.

Important:
Primarily used internally and optionally surfaced visually.

### Second Chances

A discovery category containing previously Passed vehicles.

Purpose:
Allow users to reconsider vehicles without reintroducing clutter into primary discovery.

### In Your Budget

A recommendation category focused primarily on financial compatibility.

Purpose:
Expose users to viable alternatives they may not have initially considered.

### Keep Exploring

A recommendation category used to surface additional unrated vehicles beyond the closest current matches.

Purpose:
Encourage guided exploration without overwhelming the user with the full market at once.

### All

A broader exploration category that shows the wider inventory set, including vehicles the user has already interacted with.

Purpose:
Give users a wider-market view when they want to move beyond tightly curated discovery.

### More From This Brand

A future recommendation concept showing additional vehicles from a brand the user has engaged with.

Purpose:
Encourage guided brand-based exploration.

Important:
This is a useful product concept, but it is not currently a primary surfaced tab in the app.

## Vehicle & Listing Terminology

### Vehicle

The canonical representation of a car itself.

Examples:

- Toyota Corolla 2018
- Mazda 3 2019

A Vehicle represents the actual car model/entity.

### Listing

A marketplace appearance or advertisement for a vehicle.

Examples:

- Facebook Marketplace post
- dealer inventory listing
- imported listing feed

Important:
Multiple listings may represent the same underlying vehicle.

### Seller

The individual or business listing a vehicle for sale.

Examples:

- private owner
- dealer
- reseller

### Dealer

A registered or informal business selling vehicles.

Important:
Not all sellers are dealers.

## Vehicle Classification Terminology

### Vehicle Type

A functional classification used for filtering and matching.

Examples:

- sedan
- SUV
- hatchback
- pickup
- wagon

Purpose:
Logical compatibility grouping.

### Category Label

An emotional or merchandising grouping.

Examples:

- Family Friendly
- Adventure Ready
- City Cruiser
- Budget Friendly

Purpose:
Enhance emotional discovery and browsing.

### Brand Name

The manufacturer of the vehicle.

Examples:

- Toyota
- Honda
- Nissan
- Mazda

Preferred canonical term:
`brand_name`

### Model Name

The specific vehicle model.

Examples:

- Corolla
- Civic
- X-Trail
- Mazda 3

Preferred canonical term:
`model_name`

## Ownership Terminology

### Life Together

The ownership phase of the Rev Matched ecosystem.

Includes:

- maintenance
- reminders
- ownership tracking
- service discovery
- ongoing engagement

### Ownership Record

A stored history related to the vehicle.

Examples:

- service history
- tyre replacement
- oil changes
- repairs
- inspections

### Service Provider

A business or individual providing automotive-related services.

Examples:

- mechanics
- tyre shops
- detailers
- body shops
- electricians

## Seller Journey Terminology

### Moving On

The seller phase of the Rev Matched ecosystem.

Represents:

- preparing a vehicle for sale
- listing
- pricing
- buyer engagement
- ownership transfer

### Listing Visibility

Analytics indicating how much exposure a listing is receiving.

Examples:

- views
- saves
- likes
- engagement activity

### Pricing Guidance

Suggested pricing support based on market comparisons and trends.

Purpose:
Help sellers price more competitively and realistically.

## UX & Emotional Design Terminology

### Emotional Reassurance

The feeling that the system is helping guide the user toward a good decision.

Implemented through:

- Matches
- progressive narrowing
- recommendation categories
- simplified choices

### Decision Progression System

The structured journey that gradually narrows options from exploration to commitment.

Core sequence:
Define → Discover → Match → Like → Top Pick

### Progressive Complexity

A UX principle where complexity is revealed gradually rather than all at once.

Purpose:
Reduce cognitive overload.

## Technical & Data Terminology

### System Layer

Compatibility and recommendation logic generated by the platform.

Examples:

- `is_match`
- `match_score`
- `matched_at`

### User Interaction Layer

Actions explicitly performed by the user.

Examples:

- `liked_at`
- `passed_at`
- `is_top_pick`
- `top_pick_added_at`

### Stored Field

A normalized value stored in the database.

Examples:

- `mileage_value`
- `price_amount`

### Derived Field

A presentation value generated dynamically.

Examples:

- `mileage_display`
- `price_display`

## Copy & Tone Guidelines

Rev Matched language should feel:

- human
- supportive
- locally relatable
- emotionally intelligent

Avoid:

- overly technical wording
- corporate jargon
- aggressive sales language

The platform should sound like:

> a helpful guide assisting users through a major life purchase.

## Approved Core Brand Language

Preferred phrases:

- Find Your Match
- Life Together
- Moving On
- Top Picks
- Discover
- Second Chances
- In Your Budget

Preferred emotional concepts:

- compatibility
- fit
- confidence
- guidance
- clarity
- connection

## Current UI Label Notes

To avoid confusion between broader product language and current interface labels:

- `Find Your Match` is the broader buyer-journey experience name.
- `Find the One` is the current UI/screen label used in the product navigation.

Until the product team decides otherwise, both may appear, but they should be treated as related labels rather than separate product concepts.

## Terms To Avoid

| Avoid | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Saved Cars | Liked Cars |
| Favorites | Liked Cars or Top Picks, depending on meaning |
| Search Results | Discover |
| Rejected | Passed |
| Matched (for user-facing finalist language) | Top Pick |
| Marketplace Only | Relationship Platform |

## Closing Principle

Every term in Rev Matched should reinforce the idea that:

- buying a car is emotional
- ownership is ongoing
- the platform exists to guide users through the entire relationship lifecycle with their vehicle
